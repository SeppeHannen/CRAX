The fifteen arms again, the policy seeing pixels: the staged curriculum under λ(ω) gives the best policy of either run, the uniform and PLR told arms lose the return they had, and every arm now swings between evaluations

Done 2026-10-17 on Snellius: 15 arms in parallel, 5 h 30–44 each on an A100, ~10,900 SBU. W&B group goal_point_learners_vision_500M, one seed, commit 6bc54c7 (branch contextual-review). The same arms, flags and budget as 2026-10-15_goal_point_learners_500M.md, with the observation changed; that report is the comparison throughout.

In one paragraph. With a camera next to the sensors, the per-context constraint still holds the level-3 budget where the scalar one does not (7 of 10 told arms end within 25, 2 of 5 scalar arms, one of them standing still), but the return it buys changed sides: the staged:1,2,3 told arms, unreadable on 2026-10-15 and now clean, end at 19–20 goals on level 3 at cost 20–28 and 23–24 goals on Ω — the best policies of either experiment — while the uniform and PLR told arms, the best of 2026-10-15, end at 2–12 goals and none of the eight is both above 8 goals and under budget. Every arm's evaluations swing two to five times further between consecutive evaluations than two days ago, far more than the sampling error of 128 episodes, and the multiplier oscillates with a 20–40 M-step period: the frozen policy is a different policy at every evaluation. This run changed two things at once — the observation, and (through 1024 environments instead of 8192) the number of multiplier updates per environment step, eight times more — and cannot say which did what; the next run must separate them.

Question

2026-10-15 ended with the learner settled (per-context λ(ω), told policy) and a failure it could only make rarer, not remove: the lidar cannot tell a flat disc from a solid cylinder, so the policy presses into walls it cannot see as walls (plan item 7). A camera carries that fact. Does the same experiment from pixels plus sensors keep the budget on level 3, and does the return under the budget go up now that the observation contains what decides the costly episodes? The normaliser fix (plan item 2) is in this run, so the two staged-told rows are read for the first time.

  • The swing is not evaluation noise. The standard error of a 128-episode mean is 0.1–0.6 goals in both runs; the evaluation-to-evaluation change is 1.3–5 goals here, 2.5 or more on thirteen of the fifteen arms. The frozen policy evaluated at 400 M is a different policy from the one at 425 M.
  • The multiplier oscillates at the evaluation cadence. λ's dominant period is 16–43 M steps on every arm; evaluations are 25 M apart. A series sampled at about its own period looks random, which is what the line plots show. The training cost swings 10–50 around the budget with it (multiplier.png, lower row).
  • The dual ascent runs eight times faster relative to the policy than on 2026-10-15. The multiplier steps once per round at rate 1 in both runs; a round is 81 920 steps here against 655 360, so per environment step the integrator moves eight times as far while the policy, at the same SGD steps per environment step, moves as before. An integrator that overshoots and corrects every ~30 M steps is what the λ series shows. This is a mechanism consistent with every row of the table; it is not shown to be the cause, because the observation changed in the same run (next section).
  • λ at the hardest context runs to 15–58 and ends at 12–35 on every uniform and PLR told arm (2026-10-15: peaks 5.7–10.4, ends 1.5–5), while the batch mean λ ends at 0.7–1.9 as before (PLR cost network: 4.2, the arm that stands still). The function is holding the dense corner of Ω at a price ten times the mean; the two forms no longer order (additive peaks 15–58, network 24–57). PLR's intended mass is 12–16 hazards, as before.

What this run cannot say, and what it can

  • Two causes, one run. The observation changed and the multiplier's clock changed. The swings, the high λ at the dense corner, the told arms' lost return and the scalar arms' changed end states are each consistent with a multiplier that overshoots; the staged arms' success is consistent with a curriculum that gives the fast integrator one context at a time to settle on. None of this needs the pixels, and nothing here isolates what the pixels did. The report's title is about what happened, not why.
  • What the pixels verifiably did: the policy is given them (the records hold the frames; training.review, camera policy: pixels/vision), the encoder's latent reaches every head, and the composition trains at 26.8 k SPS. Whether the policy uses them — whether the wall-pressing episodes of 2026-10-16 are gone — is a question for the records of the staged additive arm, not yet read.
  • The normaliser fix is verified on the one row that could show it.

What this changes in the plan

  1. State the multiplier's rate per environment step, not per round. --lagrangian_coef_rate is applied once per round; a run with eight times the rounds gets eight times the dual ascent. The flag should mean the same thing at every batch shape — scale the per-round step by the round's size, or declare the rate per 1 M steps — and the design doc should say so.
  2. Separate the two causes before reading the pixels. One rerun of the fifteen arms from pixels with the integrator's per-step rate matched to 2026-10-15 (rate 1/8 per round at 1024 environments); if the swings and the high λ go, the observation is read from that run and this one is the rate-change control. ~11 000 SBU; ~22 000 remain (the account's ledger has not yet booked today's jobs).
  3. Read the staged additive told records (252 episodes with frames): are the catastrophic episodes still wall-pressing with a visible wall in frame? The replay tool can also run its 20-goal checkpoint for 384 episodes on level 3 as on 2026-10-16, for the per-episode distribution.
  4. The staged curriculum is back in the comparison. With a told policy that can read its context from the start, staged:1,2,3 is the best arm of either run; with the swings unexplained it is one seed of one arm. Seeds after item 2.
  5. Report the trajectory, not the end state, while arms oscillate. The end-state table averages five evaluations of an oscillating policy; trajectories.html is the honest figure for this run and belongs in every report whose arms move between evaluations. The over-budget-fraction metric (2026-10-15 item 6) is still owed.
